abstract: >-
  [Purpose/Significance] While generative artificial intelligence (AI) enhances the efficiency of academic research, it also gives rise to risks such as data privacy concerns, algorithmic bias, fabricated findings, and blurred boundaries of responsibility. This study aims to explore task decomposition, capability boundaries, and collaborative mechanisms for embedding large language models (LLMs) into scholarly peer review, so as to improve the structured, trustworthy, and process-oriented governance of academic evaluation in the digital-intelligent environment. [Method/Process] Drawing on comprehensive evaluation theory, the human-AI collaboration framework, and component-based thinking, this study transforms scholarly peer review into composable and reviewable task components according to the logic of “evaluation domain-evaluation module-task component”. An intelligent peer-review framework is then constructed around three evaluation domains: content quality, compliance and trustworthiness, and actor-process evaluation. An exploratory validation is conducted using ACL papers and real peer-review comments. [Result/Conclusion] The results show that process componentization can transform LLM outputs into intermediate review materials, thereby supporting content understanding, risk identification, and feedback organization. LLMs should support, rather than replace, expert judgment by providing process-oriented assistance in scholarly peer review. [Innovation/Limitation] This study proposes a component-based operational framework for LLMs-assisted scholarly peer review and extends the application of comprehensive evaluation theory in intelligent review scenarios. Due to limitations in sample size, expert databases, and revision materials, future research should conduct larger-scale validation and explore process-oriented application scenarios.
